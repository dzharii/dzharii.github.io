## Article notes

[^1]: **1. Teach Yourself Programming in Ten Years** - Peter Norvig. Craft expertise takes years; learn by doing, reading code, collaborating, and working across multiple paradigms. [Open article](https://norvig.com/21-days.html).
[^2]: **2. No Silver Bullet: Essence and Accidents of Software Engineering** - Frederick P. Brooks Jr.. Separates essential from accidental complexity and argues that no single tool or technique can produce an order-of-magnitude leap in software productivity. [Open article](https://www.cs.unc.edu/techreports/86-020.pdf).
[^3]: **3. Programming as Theory Building** - Peter Naur. Programs are embodied theories in programmers' heads; maintenance succeeds when that understanding is transferred, not merely when source code survives. [Open article](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~remzi/Naur.pdf).
[^4]: **4. The Law of Leaky Abstractions** - Joel Spolsky. Useful abstractions eventually expose their underlying machinery at boundaries, failures, or performance limits. [Open article](https://www.joelonsoftware.com/2002/11/11/the-law-of-leaky-abstractions/).
[^5]: **5. Things You Should Never Do, Part I** - Joel Spolsky. Full rewrites discard accumulated bug fixes and domain knowledge; incremental repair is usually less glamorous and more valuable. [Open article](https://www.joelonsoftware.com/2000/04/06/things-you-should-never-do-part-i/).
[^6]: **6. Out of the Tar Pit** - Ben Moseley and Peter Marks. Argues that complexity, particularly mutable state and complicated control, is software's central enemy and proposes ways to minimize both. [Open article](https://curtclifton.net/papers/MoseleyMarks06a.pdf).
[^7]: **7. You and Your Research** - Richard Hamming. A durable guide to choosing important problems, cultivating courage, creating favorable working conditions, and maximizing research impact. [Open article](https://www.cs.virginia.edu/~robins/YouAndYourResearch.html).
[^8]: **8. The Rise of 'Worse is Better'** - Richard P. Gabriel. Explains why simple, implementation-friendly systems can spread and improve faster than designs that pursue theoretical perfection. [Open article](https://www.dreamsongs.com/RiseOfWorseIsBetter.html).
[^9]: **9. Reflections on Trusting Trust** - Ken Thompson. The classic compiler-backdoor argument showing that trust in software cannot ultimately be reduced to inspection of source code alone. [Open article](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~rdriley/487/papers/Thompson_1984_ReflectionsonTrustingTrust.pdf).
[^10]: **10. How Complex Systems Fail** - Richard I. Cook. Complex-system failures are emergent and multi-causal; useful incident analysis must resist convenient single-root-cause stories. [Open article](https://how.complexsystems.fail/).
[^11]: **11. Choose Boring Technology** - Dan McKinley. Treat novelty as a scarce budget: mature technology has known failure modes, while every unfamiliar component consumes operational learning capacity. [Open article](https://mcfunley.com/choose-boring-technology).
[^12]: **12. The Grug Brained Developer** - Carson Gross. A deliberately comic but incisive case that complexity compounds quickly and that simple, explicit designs preserve engineering cognition. [Open article](https://grugbrain.dev/).
[^13]: **13. The Wrong Abstraction** - Sandi Metz. Prefer temporary duplication to forcing divergent behavior through a bad abstraction; re-abstract only after the real variation becomes visible. [Open article](https://sandimetz.com/blog/2016/1/20/the-wrong-abstraction).
[^14]: **14. Parse, Don't Validate** - Alexis King. Move validation to system boundaries by parsing raw inputs into richer types, so invalid states become difficult or impossible to represent. [Open article](https://lexi-lambda.github.io/blog/2019/11/05/parse-don-t-validate/).
[^15]: **15. End-to-End Arguments in System Design** - Jerome H. Saltzer, David P. Reed, and David D. Clark. Correctness properties often belong at application endpoints because lower layers cannot completely guarantee the semantics applications actually need. [Open article](https://web.mit.edu/Saltzer/www/publications/endtoend/endtoend.pdf).
[^16]: **16. A Note on Distributed Computing** - Jim Waldo, Geoff Wyant, Ann Wollrath, and Sam Kendall. Remote calls are fundamentally unlike local calls because latency, partial failure, concurrency, and object lifetime cross a semantic boundary. [Open article](https://waldo.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/note-distributed-computing).
[^17]: **17. On the Criteria To Be Used in Decomposing Systems into Modules** - David L. Parnas. Information hiding and likely change boundaries are better decomposition criteria than simply mirroring the sequence of processing steps. [Open article](https://prl.khoury.northeastern.edu/img/p-tr-1971.pdf).
[^18]: **18. The Humble Programmer** - Edsger W. Dijkstra. Programming demands humility before complexity and languages that reduce the intellectual distance between program text and program behavior. [Open article](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~EWD/transcriptions/EWD03xx/EWD340.html).
[^19]: **19. Go To Statement Considered Harmful** - Edsger W. Dijkstra. The famous argument that unconstrained jumps make program progress harder to reason about and that structure improves comprehensibility and proof. [Open article](https://homepages.cwi.nl/~storm/teaching/reader/Dijkstra68.pdf).
[^20]: **20. Why Functional Programming Matters** - John Hughes. Makes the enduring case that higher-order functions and lazy evaluation are modularity tools, not merely language-style preferences. [Open article](https://www.cs.kent.ac.uk/people/staff/dat/miranda/whyfp90.pdf).
[^21]: **21. Big Ball of Mud** - Brian Foote and Joseph Yoder. Catalogs the forces that cause architecture to decay into sprawling tangles and explains why such systems persist despite their obvious defects. [Open article](http://www.laputan.org/mud/).
[^22]: **22. CAP Twelve Years Later: How the 'Rules' Have Changed** - Eric Brewer. Refines the simplified CAP slogan: partitions force tradeoffs, but practical systems make nuanced choices across operations and over time. [Open article](https://www.infoq.com/articles/cap-twelve-years-later-how-the-rules-have-changed/).
[^23]: **23. Life Beyond Distributed Transactions: An Apostate's Opinion** - Pat Helland. Large systems often cannot depend on global transactions; design instead around messages, entities, local decisions, and uncertainty. [Open article](https://www.cidrdb.org/cidr2007/papers/cidr07p15.pdf).
[^24]: **24. Building on Quicksand** - Pat Helland and David Campbell. Warns against treating shared mutable state and perfect agreement as stable foundations for distributed applications. [Open article](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.1788).
[^25]: **25. Immutability Changes Everything** - Pat Helland. Cheap storage changes system economics: immutable facts and histories reduce coordination pressure and make recomputation and replication easier. [Open article](https://www.cidrdb.org/cidr2015/Papers/CIDR15_Paper16.pdf).
[^26]: **26. The Log: What every software engineer should know about real-time data's unifying abstraction** - Jay Kreps. Shows how the append-only log unifies database replication, stream processing, change capture, and data integration. [Open article](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/distributed-systems/log-what-every-software-engineer-should-know-about-real-time-datas-unifying).
[^27]: **27. Scalability! But at what COST?** - Frank McSherry, Michael Isard, and Derek G. Murray. Scalability claims are incomplete without competent single-thread baselines; parallelism can conceal huge overheads rather than demonstrate speed. [Open article](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/conference/hotos15/hotos15-paper-mcsherry.pdf).
[^28]: **28. The Tail at Scale** - Jeffrey Dean and Luiz Andre Barroso. At large fan-out, user-visible latency is governed by slow outliers, making redundancy, hedging, and tail-aware design first-class concerns. [Open article](https://research.google/pubs/the-tail-at-scale/).
[^29]: **29. Ironies of Automation** - Lisanne Bainbridge. Automation can make operators less practiced while leaving them responsible for the hardest anomalies, creating a fundamental human-machine paradox. [Open article](https://ckrybus.com/static/papers/Bainbridge_1983_Automatica.pdf).
[^30]: **30. An Investigation of the Therac-25 Accidents** - Nancy G. Leveson and Clark S. Turner. A canonical safety case study showing how software defects, interface design, testing, process, and organizational assumptions can combine catastrophically. [Open article](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/5dr206s3).
[^31]: **31. They Write the Right Stuff** - Charles Fishman. NASA shuttle software illustrates how exceptional reliability emerges from disciplined process, review, conservative change, and organizational practice. [Open article](https://www.fastcompany.com/28121/they-write-right-stuff).
